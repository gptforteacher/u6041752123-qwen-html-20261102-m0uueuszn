Present Continuous

I am doing
🤔 Think about it:
What are you doing right now — and what did you start doing this week but haven't finished yet?

Unit 1 · English Grammar in Use

🚗 Look at this situation

🚗

Sarah is in her car.

She is on her way to work.

She 's driving to work.

This means: she is driving now, at the time of speaking.

The action is not finished.

What are YOU doing right now? (sitting? listening? thinking?)

📐 The Formula

am / is / are + verb-ing
This is the Present Continuous
I
am driving (= I'm)
He / She / It
is working (= he's)
We / You / They
are doing (= we're)
Examples
reading, working, playing

Make a sentence about yourself using "I am + -ing". What are you doing at this moment?

📱 A tricky situation

📱

Steve is talking to a friend on the phone.

He says: "I'm reading a really good book."

⚠️ But Steve is NOT reading the book at the moment of speaking!

💡 He means: he has started reading the book, but has not finished it yet.

He is in the middle of reading it.

What TV series are you watching these days? (You're not watching it right now, but you've started it!)

⏳ The Key Idea

I am doing something =
I started doing it and I haven't finished;
I'm in the middle of doing it.
🟢 STARTED
in the past
⚡ NOW
in the middle
🔴 NOT FINISHED
in the future

Look at these examples:

Now
Please don't make noise. I'm trying to work.
Now
Where's Mark? He's having a shower.
Now
Let's go out. It isn't raining any more.
Now
What's all that noise? What is happening?

🌍 Not always at this exact moment

Sometimes the action is not happening right now, but it's in progress in your life.

🇮🇹

Kate wants to work in Italy,

so she's learning Italian.

(but perhaps she isn't learning Italian at this exact moment)

🏠 Some friends of mine are building their own house.

They hope to finish it next summer.

What are you learning these days? (a language? a skill? a musical instrument?)

📅 Periods around NOW

We use Present Continuous with:

today
this week
this year
this month
Today
You're working hard today.
(not "You work hard today")
This year
The company I work for isn't doing so well this year.

What are you working on this week? What's happening in your life today?

📈 Talking about CHANGES

We use Present Continuous when something has started to change.

We often use these verbs:

getting
becoming
changing
improving
starting
beginning
increasing
rising
falling
growing
Example 1
Is your English getting better?
(not "Does your English get better?")
Example 2
The population of the world is increasing very fast.
Example 3
At first I didn't like my job, but I'm starting to enjoy it now.
Example 4
Look at those clouds! It's getting dark.

📝 Summary

🔹 Form: am / is / are + verb-ing

🔹 Meaning: action in progress, not finished

🔹 Use for:

  • actions happening NOW
  • actions in progress around now (today, this week)
  • changes (getting, becoming, improving...)
